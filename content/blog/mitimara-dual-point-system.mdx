# Understanding {siteName}'s Dual-Point System: Ecosystem & Policing Points

At {siteName}, we've designed a unique dual-point system that rewards different types of positive contributions to our tree-planting platform. This blog post explains how our ecosystem points and policing points work, how you earn them, and how they translate into real value through Pi token exchanges.

## Ecosystem Points: Fueling Tree Planting and Verification

### What Are Ecosystem Points?

Ecosystem points are at the core of our reward system, designed to make tree planting impactful and engaging. These points drive the primary activities on our platform: [planting Trees](/blog/how-to-plant-a-tree) and [verifying others' plantings](/blog/how-to-plant-a-tree).

### How to Earn and Use Ecosystem Points

Our ecosystem points system is designed to be simple yet effective:

- **Starting Balance**: You receive 10 ecosystem points when you sign up.
- **Planting Trees**: It costs 5 points to plant a tree.
- **Verifying Trees**: It costs 3 points to verify a tree planted by another user.
- **Successful Authentication**: When your planted tree or verification is authenticated, you get double your points back! This creates a net gain for honest participants.
- **Purchasing Points**: Need extra points? You can subscribe to purchase more at a rate of 1 Pi token = 100 ecosystem points.

### The Value of Ecosystem Points

What makes ecosystem points truly valuable is their exchangeability for Pi tokens. We use a gamified formula to ensure fair distribution:

```plaintext
Your Pi Reward = (Your Points / Total Users' Points) * Amount of Pi Tokens in the Planter/Verifier Pot
```

This proportional system ensures that the most active and honest participants receive the largest share of rewards, creating a positive incentive loop that benefits the entire community.

## Policing Points: [Maintaining Platform Integrity](/blog/introducing-mitimara-community-policing)

### What Are Policing Points?

While ecosystem points drive planting and verification, policing points serve a different but equally important purpose: maintaining the integrity of our platform through community oversight.

### How to Earn and Use Policing Points

Policing points work differently from ecosystem points:

- **Starting Balance**: Every user starts with 1 policing point.
- **Using Points**: Submitting a tree report costs 1 policing point.
- **Earning Points**: When admins agree with your report (e.g., a fake tree you reported is confirmed fake, or your contest of a "fake" marking is validated), you earn 2 policing points.
- **Net Gain/Loss**: This creates a net gain (+1) for accurate reports and a net loss (-1) for inaccurate ones.

### The Value of Policing Points

Just like ecosystem points, policing points can be exchanged for Pi tokens using a similar gamified formula:

```plaintext
Your Pi Reward = (Your Policing Points / Total Users' Policing Points) * Amount of Pi Tokens in the Policing Pot
```

This system rewards users who actively participate in maintaining platform integrity, creating a financial incentive to help police the ecosystem.

## How The Dual-Point System Creates a Balanced Ecosystem

Our dual-point system creates a balanced ecosystem in several ways:

1. **Specialization**: Users can focus on planting, verifying, policing, or all three based on their preferences and skills.
2. **Multiple Reward Paths**: Different ways to contribute mean different ways to earn.
3. **Self-Regulation**: The community has financial incentives to maintain its own integrity.
4. **Balanced Incentives**: The system discourages both false tree claims and false reporting.

## Example Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Tree Planter

Maria focuses primarily on planting trees. She plants 10 trees (using 50 ecosystem points) and 8 are verified as authentic. She receives double points back for these 8 trees (80 points), creating a net gain of 30 points. When exchange time comes, her proportion of ecosystem points determines her Pi token reward from the Planter/Verifier Pot.

### Scenario 2: The Community Police Officer

John specializes in identifying problematic tree submissions. He successfully reports 5 falsely verified trees (using 5 policing points) and the admin agrees with all his reports. He earns 10 policing points back, for a net gain of 5 points. His proportion of policing points determines his reward from the Policing Pot.
